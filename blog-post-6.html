What "Soulslike" Actually Means

The term "soulslike" was born from Dark Souls — a game that fundamentally changed how we think about difficulty in gaming. It's not about cheap difficulty spikes or unfair boss patterns. It's about a fair challenge that demands respect.

A true soulslike has these core principles:

  • Skill matters more than stats. Your character's equipment shouldn't determine victory — your ability to read patterns and react does.
  • Every boss is learnable. No RNG nonsense. No "this boss one-shots you randomly." Every attack has a tell. Every pattern can be mastered.
  • Progression is about mastery, not grinding. You don't get stronger by farming mobs for 10 hours. You get stronger by understanding, adapting, and executing.
  • Death is feedback, not punishment. When you die, you learn. That's the whole design.

Ashfall: Pure Soulslike Design

Many games slap the "soulslike" label on themselves while violating every principle above. They ask you to grind. They add pay-to-win mechanics. They hide difficulty behind stat multiplication.

Ashfall doesn't.
